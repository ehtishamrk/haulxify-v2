1. What Is Transport Back Office Support?

Transport back office support refers to all the administrative, operational, and compliance functions that keep a transport business running — everything that happens off the road. While drivers handle pickups and deliveries, the back office handles the paperwork, billing, scheduling, regulatory filings, and communication that make those trips possible and profitable.

The term "back office" comes from the physical layout of older businesses — the front office handled customer-facing work, while the back office handled internal operations. In transport, the distinction is between driving (front office) and everything else (back office).

For most small transport businesses, the back office is either handled by the owner personally — taking time away from operations and revenue-generating activities — or by one or two employees who become single points of failure when they are sick, take vacation, or leave the company.

2. What Does Transport Back Office Support Include?

Transport back office support encompasses a broad range of functions. Providers vary in which services they offer — some specialize in dispatch only, while others offer comprehensive back office coverage across all functions. The core services are:

Dispatch and Load Management

Finding loads, negotiating rates with brokers, reviewing and accepting rate confirmations, booking shipments, assigning drivers, communicating pickup and delivery instructions, and tracking loads in transit. Dispatch is typically the highest-frequency back office function — it happens multiple times per day.

Billing and Invoicing

Preparing and submitting invoices to brokers, shippers, or direct customers. Collecting signed bills of lading (BOLs) and proofs of delivery (PODs). Following up on outstanding payments. Managing factoring company submissions and reconciliation. Billing is the direct link between work completed and cash received — delays or errors here directly impact cash flow.

Document Processing

Collecting, organizing, and processing the paperwork generated by every load — rate confirmations, BOLs, PODs, lumper receipts, weight tickets, accessorial documentation. For a 5-truck fleet running 4 loads per truck per week, this is 80+ documents per week that must be collected, filed, and retrievable.

IFTA Filing

Quarterly filing of the International Fuel Tax Agreement return. This requires tracking state-by-state mileage throughout the quarter, collecting and reconciling fuel receipts, calculating net tax liability per jurisdiction, and submitting the return to the base jurisdiction by the quarterly deadline. Late filings trigger minimum $50 penalties plus interest.

DOT Compliance Management

Maintaining driver qualification files, tracking medical certificate expirations, managing drug and alcohol testing program requirements, monitoring hours of service compliance, maintaining vehicle maintenance records, and tracking permit and license renewal dates. DOT compliance is ongoing and deadline-driven — missed deadlines create violations that follow the carrier's safety record.

Bookkeeping

Recording income and expenses, maintaining the chart of accounts, reconciling bank statements, preparing financial reports, and supporting tax preparation. Some back office providers include basic bookkeeping in their service; others offer it as an add-on.

Driver Hiring and HR Support

Posting job listings, screening applications, verifying CDL and driving records, conducting background checks, managing pre-employment drug testing, and maintaining employment records. For growing fleets, driver hiring is a recurring back office demand that takes significant time.

After-Hours Coverage

Answering calls and managing dispatch outside of standard business hours. Particularly critical for tow truck operators who receive calls around the clock, and for any carrier running overnight or cross-country freight where driver issues can arise at any hour.

3. Which Transport Industries Use Back Office Support?

Back office support is used across virtually every segment of the transport industry. The specific functions required vary by industry — a tow truck company needs 24/7 dispatch and motor club billing, while a freight broker needs carrier vetting and POD management — but the underlying need for administrative support is universal.

Trucking Companies

Owner-operators and small fleets — dispatch, IFTA filing, DOT compliance, billing, factoring coordination.

Freight Brokers

Carrier vetting, POD management, load documentation, after-hours check calls, TMS support.

Tow Truck Companies

24/7 dispatch, motor club billing (AAA, Agero), insurance dispute documentation, impound paperwork.

NEMT Providers

Medicaid billing, trip documentation, eligibility verification, authorization tracking, denial management.

Taxi Fleets

Dispatch, corporate account billing, permit renewal tracking, driver scheduling.

Limousine Companies

24/7 reservation management, corporate account billing, driver coordination, after-hours coverage.

Car Haulers

BOL and vehicle condition report management, Super Dispatch integration, OEM and auction billing.

Dump Truck Operators

Per-load and per-ton billing, digital ticket processing, subcontractor pay reconciliation.

Sprinter & Cargo Van

Load board monitoring, dispatch, billing, route optimization for last-mile and courier freight.

Moving Companies

Job scheduling, crew management, quote handling, billing for residential and corporate moves.

Animal Haulers

USDA permit management, CVI certificate tracking, live cargo DOT compliance documentation.

Moving Truck Operators

Scheduling, double-booking prevention, billing, seasonal hiring support.

5. In-House vs Outsourced Back Office — The Real Comparison

The decision to hire in-house or outsource back office functions is one of the most consequential operational decisions a transport business makes. Both approaches have legitimate advantages — the right choice depends on fleet size, growth stage, and operational complexity.

The True Cost of In-House Back Office

Most operators calculate in-house back office cost as the employee's salary. The actual all-in cost is 1.3 to 1.4 times salary after payroll taxes, health insurance, workers' compensation, paid time off, and training costs. For a mid-level back office employee in the US earning $48,000, the all-in annual cost is typically $63,000 to $72,000.

Additional in-house costs that are frequently overlooked: recruitment cost when the employee leaves (average back office turnover in logistics is 28% annually), the productivity gap during the replacement period, and the operational disruption caused by a single point of failure when the employee is sick or on vacation.

The Advantages of Outsourcing

Outsourced back office eliminates hiring, training, and retention risk. The provider is responsible for staffing continuity — your operations are not disrupted when an individual leaves. Outsourcing also provides immediate scalability: adding trucks means adjusting a service agreement, not recruiting and training a new employee over 60 to 90 days.

For small fleets of 1 to 10 trucks, the cost differential between outsourced and in-house is substantial enough that the financial case for outsourcing is almost always clear. For larger operations, the calculation becomes more nuanced based on whether dedicated in-house staff provides operational advantages that justify the cost premium.

6. Can Transport Back Office Be Handled Offshore?

Offshore transport back office support — provided by teams in countries like Pakistan, India, and the Philippines — has become a mainstream operational choice for US transport businesses of all sizes. The cost advantage is significant: offshore back office typically costs 60 to 75 percent less than equivalent US-based services.

The historical concern about offshore transport back office — that teams outside the US would not understand the regulatory environment, load boards, TMS platforms, and operational culture of US freight — has been largely addressed by specialized training programs and years of operational experience serving US clients.

What to evaluate when considering offshore back office:

  • US transport-specific training: Does the team have documented experience with FMCSA regulations, IFTA, major US load boards (DAT, Truckstop), TMS platforms (McLeod, ITS Dispatch, TruckLogics), and factoring companies?
  • English language proficiency: Back office functions require written and verbal communication with US brokers, shippers, and drivers. Assess communication quality before committing.
  • Time zone coverage: Pakistan-based operations (UTC+5) overlap with US overnight and early morning hours — a natural advantage for after-hours coverage. India and Philippines have similar or favorable overlap depending on which US time zones you operate in.
  • Dedicated vs shared staff: A dedicated team member who learns your operation produces significantly better results than a shared pool handling multiple clients simultaneously.
  • Data security: NDA agreements, role-based access controls, and documented data handling procedures are non-negotiable for offshore arrangements handling carrier data, financial information, and driver records.

7. How to Choose a Transport Back Office Provider

Choosing a back office provider is a significant operational decision. The wrong choice creates more problems than it solves — a provider who does not understand your industry, cannot communicate reliably, or does not have adequate staffing continuity will cost you more in errors and recovery time than their service fee saves.

Key questions to ask every provider before committing:

  • Do I have a dedicated team member or am I in a shared pool?
  • What is your staff turnover rate and what happens to my account when someone leaves?
  • Which TMS platforms, load boards, and software systems have you worked with?
  • Can I speak directly with my team and via which channels?
  • What is your error correction process when something goes wrong?
  • What are your contract terms and what notice is required to cancel?
  • Can you provide references from current clients in my transport segment?
  • How do you handle IFTA specifically — do you prepare and file, or just track?
  • What hours are your team available and how is after-hours coverage handled?

Red flags to watch for: vague answers about "our team" without specifics, inability to name TMS platforms they have used, no clear answer on what happens when your team member leaves, and minimum contract periods longer than 3 months for a new client relationship.

8. IFTA Filing — What It Is and Who Handles It

The International Fuel Tax Agreement (IFTA) is a compact between 48 US states and 10 Canadian provinces that simplifies fuel tax reporting for interstate commercial carriers. Instead of filing separate fuel tax returns in every state they operate in, carriers file one quarterly return with their base jurisdiction.

IFTA applies to any power unit with two axles and a gross vehicle weight over 26,000 lbs, or three or more axles regardless of weight, operating in two or more IFTA jurisdictions. Filing deadlines are April 30, July 31, October 31, and January 31 for the preceding quarter.

IFTA filing requires quarterly collection of state-by-state mileage data, fuel purchase records organized by state, calculation of net tax liability per jurisdiction, and submission of the return to the base jurisdiction. Late filings trigger a minimum $50 penalty or 10% of net tax due, whichever is greater.

Back office providers who include IFTA support typically handle tracking throughout the quarter and file the return by the deadline. The quality difference between providers is significant — some only compile records you provide, while others actively manage mileage and fuel tracking to ensure audit-ready documentation.

9. DOT Compliance — What Back Office Covers

DOT compliance management is one of the most time-intensive ongoing back office functions for carriers operating under their own authority. The core compliance requirements that back office support addresses:

  • Driver Qualification Files: Maintaining complete DQFs for every driver — employment application, MVR, medical certificate, road test certificate, previous employer safety history, and annual review documentation.
  • Medical certificate tracking: Monitoring expiration dates and triggering renewals before certificates lapse. An expired MEC means the driver cannot legally operate.
  • Drug and alcohol testing program: Managing random testing pool requirements, coordinating pre-employment tests, and maintaining testing records. FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse queries are required for new hires and annually for current drivers.
  • Annual vehicle inspections: Tracking inspection dates and ensuring current inspection certificates are on all vehicles before they expire.
  • UCR and operating authority: Annual Unified Carrier Registration and periodic MCS-150 updates to keep operating authority current.
  • Hours of service monitoring: For operations not yet fully on ELD, back office support includes monitoring driver log compliance.

10. Dispatch vs Full Back Office — What Is the Difference?

Dispatch and back office are frequently used interchangeably but they refer to different scopes of service. Understanding the distinction helps carriers identify what they actually need.

Dispatch specifically refers to load finding, rate negotiation, booking, and driver coordination. A dispatcher finds loads on DAT or Truckstop, calls the broker, negotiates the rate, sends the rate confirmation to the driver, and tracks the load through delivery. Dispatch is a subset of back office.

Full back office includes dispatch plus all the functions that happen after the load delivers — billing, document collection, IFTA tracking, compliance management, bookkeeping, and administrative support. A back office provider takes the full operational load off the carrier, not just the load-finding component.

Many carriers start with dispatch-only services and later transition to full back office as they grow. The inflection point is typically around 3 to 5 trucks — at that point the billing, compliance, and document volume requires dedicated administrative time that exceeds what a dispatch-only arrangement covers.
