DOT Compliance Checklist for Small Trucking Companies

Haulxify Team · March 11, 2025 · 11 min read

DOT compliance is one of those areas where small trucking companies consistently fall behind — not because the requirements are unclear, but because the documentation work is relentless and rarely feels urgent until a roadside inspection or audit makes it suddenly very urgent. This checklist covers the core compliance requirements for small fleets and owner-operators operating under their own authority.

$16,000
Maximum civil penalty per violation for hours-of-service infractions under FMCSA regulations. Willful violations can exceed this significantly. Most small fleet owners do not know this number until they receive a notice.

Driver Qualification File (DQF) Requirements

Every driver you employ or contract must have a complete Driver Qualification File before they operate a commercial vehicle under your authority. The DQF must contain:

  • Completed employment application
  • Motor Vehicle Record (MVR) — obtained from the state of licensure
  • Previous employer safety performance history (3 years)
  • Road test certificate or equivalent documentation
  • Medical Examiner's Certificate (valid MEC from a registered examiner)
  • CDL copy — verified against state records
  • Annual review of driving record
  • Signed list of violations (annual)

DQFs must be retained for the duration of employment plus 3 years after termination. The annual MVR review and violation list are the two most commonly missed ongoing requirements. Calendar them at the start of each year for every driver.

The Medical Examiner's Certificate must be from an examiner listed on the FMCSA National Registry. Certificates from non-registered examiners are invalid regardless of when they were issued. Verify every examiner at nationalregistry.fmcsa.dot.gov.

Vehicle Maintenance File Requirements

Every commercial vehicle in your fleet requires a maintenance file containing:

  • Vehicle identification — make, model, year, VIN, license plate
  • Record of all inspections, repairs, and maintenance performed
  • Annual vehicle inspection — must be performed by a qualified inspector and the current inspection certificate displayed on the vehicle
  • Driver Vehicle Inspection Reports (DVIRs) — retained for 3 months

The annual inspection is one of the most commonly cited violations at roadside inspections. An expired annual inspection sticker is visible from outside the cab and is the first thing an officer checks.

Hours of Service (HOS) Compliance

HOS rules govern how many hours a driver can operate before mandatory rest. For property-carrying drivers, the core rules are:

  • 11-Hour Driving Limit: May drive a maximum of 11 hours after 10 consecutive hours off duty
  • 14-Hour Limit: May not drive beyond the 14th consecutive hour after coming on duty, following 10 hours off duty
  • 30-Minute Break: Required after 8 cumulative hours of driving without at least a 30-minute break
  • 60/70-Hour Limit: May not drive after 60/70 hours on duty in 7/8 consecutive days
  • Sleeper Berth Provision: Drivers using a sleeper berth may split their required 10-hour off-duty period

Since the ELD mandate, most carriers are compliant on HOS tracking — the ELD handles it automatically. The compliance failure now typically comes from drivers manually editing logs without proper annotations, or from yard moves and personal conveyance being misused.

Drug and Alcohol Testing Program

Every carrier operating under FMCSA authority must have a DOT drug and alcohol testing program in place. This includes:

  • Pre-employment testing for all new CDL drivers
  • Random testing — minimum 50% of average driver count for drugs, 10% for alcohol annually
  • Post-accident testing — required after accidents meeting specific criteria
  • Reasonable suspicion testing when a trained supervisor observes behavioral indicators
  • Return-to-duty and follow-up testing after any violation

You must either manage this program yourself through a consortium or use a third-party TPA (Third Party Administrator). Owner-operators must join a consortium — you cannot run your own random testing program with only one driver.

FMCSA's Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse went live January 6, 2020. You are required to query the Clearinghouse for all new hires and conduct annual queries for current drivers. Failure to query is a violation even if the driver has no violations on record.

Insurance Requirements

Minimum insurance requirements vary by operation type:

  • For-hire carriers (general freight): $750,000 minimum public liability
  • Hazardous materials: $1,000,000 to $5,000,000 depending on commodity
  • Passenger carriers (9+ passengers for compensation): $5,000,000
  • Household goods carriers: $750,000 plus cargo insurance

Your insurance certificate must be on file with FMCSA. Lapses in insurance — even brief ones — can result in operating authority revocation. Many small carriers discover this when a broker runs a carrier search and sees an authority status issue.

Operating Authority and Registration

Check these items annually or whenever you make changes to your operation:

  • MC Number status — verify active at safer.fmcsa.dot.gov
  • UCR registration — Unified Carrier Registration, required annually, fees based on fleet size
  • BOC-3 filing — process agent filing, must be current
  • MCS-150 biennial update — required every two years or when information changes
  • State operating permits — some states require additional permits for certain commodities or weight classes

Your Monthly Compliance Review Checklist

Run through this list at the start of every month:

  • Check expiration dates on all driver medical certificates
  • Verify all annual vehicle inspections are current
  • Confirm random drug testing pool is correctly maintained
  • Review any DVIRs showing defects — confirm repairs were completed and documented
  • Check that all new hires have complete DQFs before driving
  • Verify insurance certificates are current with FMCSA
  • Check Clearinghouse for any new violations on current drivers

The carriers that stay compliant are not the ones who have more knowledge — they are the ones who have built the review into a routine. A 30-minute monthly review prevents the kind of violation that costs five figures to resolve.
