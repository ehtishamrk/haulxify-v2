IFTA Filing: The Complete Guide for Owner-Operators and Small Fleets

Haulxify Team · January 14, 2025 · 9 min read

If you're an interstate motor carrier with a qualified motor vehicle operating in two or more IFTA member jurisdictions, you're required to file quarterly. Miss a filing, under-report, or miscalculate — and you're looking at penalties, interest, and potential audit flags that can follow your MC number for years.

This guide covers everything you need to file correctly, written for owner-operators and small fleets who handle their own back office.

$500+
Average penalty for a first-time IFTA non-filing infraction, before interest. Repeat failures escalate to license revocation.

What IFTA Actually Is (And Why It Matters More Than You Think)

The International Fuel Tax Agreement is a compact between 48 US states and 10 Canadian provinces that simplifies fuel tax reporting for commercial carriers. Instead of filing separate fuel tax returns in every state you operate in, you file one quarterly return with your base jurisdiction — and they distribute the tax to each state based on your reported mileage.

IFTA applies to any power unit that has two axles and a GVW or GVWR over 26,000 lbs, or has three or more axles regardless of weight. If that's your truck, you're in.

IFTA quarters: Q1 (Jan–Mar) due April 30 | Q2 (Apr–Jun) due July 31 | Q3 (Jul–Sep) due October 31 | Q4 (Oct–Dec) due January 31. Late filings carry a $50 minimum penalty or 10% of the net tax due — whichever is greater.

The Mileage Records That IFTA Auditors Actually Want to See

IFTA requires that you can document total miles driven in each jurisdiction for each quarter. This sounds simple. In practice, it's where most small operators fall apart.

Acceptable mileage documentation includes:

  • ELD records — if your ELD system produces jurisdiction-level mileage breakdowns (most do), this is your cleanest source
  • GPS trip data exported from your tracking platform with state-by-state mileage
  • Driver trip sheets filled out at every state line crossing — old school, still valid
  • Dispatch records cross-referenced with odometer logs — acceptable but creates more audit exposure

What auditors specifically look for: consistency between your odometer readings and your reported mileage. If your GPS says 14,200 miles for the quarter and your odometer difference is 18,400 miles, you have a problem.

Fuel Records: What You Can and Can't Claim

IFTA lets you claim tax paid on fuel purchased in member jurisdictions — but only if you have the documentation. Acceptable fuel receipts must show: date, seller name and address, fuel type, number of gallons, and the unit number or vehicle plate.

Card-lock receipts and fleet card statements generally meet this standard. Cash purchases at a truck stop are only creditable if you kept the receipt. Bulk fuel purchased and stored at your yard is creditable — but requires a separate tracking log showing how much was dispensed to each vehicle.

How to Calculate Your Net Tax (Without the Headache)

The IFTA calculation goes like this for each jurisdiction:

  1. Calculate average fleet MPG: total miles ÷ total gallons
  2. Calculate taxable gallons for each state: state miles ÷ average MPG
  3. Subtract gallons actually purchased in that state
  4. Multiply the difference by that state's current fuel tax rate
  5. A positive result = tax owed. A negative result = credit from that state.

Net everything out across all states. If you owe, pay your base jurisdiction. If you're in credit overall, your base jurisdiction processes the refund.

Important: fuel tax rates change. Always pull current rates from your base jurisdiction's IFTA rate chart before filing. Using last quarter's rates is a common error that triggers adjustment notices.

The 5 IFTA Mistakes That Trigger Audits

  • Inconsistent MPG across quarters. If your fleet averages 6.8 MPG every quarter for two years, then suddenly reports 9.2 MPG in Q3, that's a red flag.
  • Mileage in a state with no fuel purchases. Operating 4,000 miles in a state but claiming zero fuel purchased there gets flagged automatically.
  • Missing odometer readings. Your quarterly beginning and ending odometer must reconcile with your total miles reported.
  • Late filing. Even a zero-liability quarter must be filed. A missing filing is treated as non-compliance regardless of whether you owed anything.
  • Incorrect vehicle count. Filing for 3 trucks when you operated 4 in a quarter is a reportable discrepancy.

Software vs. Manual: What Small Fleets Actually Use

TruckingOffice, Axon Trucking, and most modern TMS platforms have built-in IFTA reporting that generates your quarterly return automatically if you're logging mileage correctly. For owner-operators, the IFTA reporting built into most ELD apps (Samsara, KeepTruckin/Motive, BigRoad) is often sufficient.

Manual spreadsheet filing is legal but creates the most audit risk because human error compounds quarterly. If you're doing it by hand, have someone else verify the numbers before you submit.

What Happens If You Get Audited

IFTA audits are conducted by your base jurisdiction and typically cover the most recent 4 years of filings. Auditors will request: all mileage records, all fuel receipts, vehicle registration documents, and driver logs.

The most common audit outcome for small operators isn't fraud — it's under-documentation. If you can't prove mileage or fuel purchases, the auditor estimates using national average figures, which almost always result in additional tax owed.

Responding to an IFTA audit without complete records costs the average small fleet $3,000–$8,000 in assessed back taxes, penalties, and interest. The records that prevent this cost nothing to maintain — they just require discipline.
