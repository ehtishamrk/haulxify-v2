Why Trucking Invoices Get Paid Late — And the Exact System to Fix It

Haulxify Team · January 21, 2025 · 7 min read

The average payment cycle in US trucking is 32 days from delivery. For small carriers, it often stretches to 45–60 days — not because brokers are slow, but because the invoice package wasn't complete, wasn't submitted the right way, or wasn't followed up on.

This is fixable. The fix isn't factoring. The fix is a disciplined invoicing system that makes it impossible for a broker or shipper to have a reason to delay payment.

days — average payment cycle for US trucking invoices. Operators with a systematic invoicing process typically collect in 7–12 days from the same brokers.

The Real Reason Invoices Get Delayed

Brokers don't delay payment because they want to. They delay payment because their accounts payable process is triggered by a complete invoice package — and most small carriers don't submit one on the first attempt.

A complete invoice package for most brokers includes: signed rate confirmation, proof of delivery (signed BOL), and the invoice itself with the correct load number referenced. If any of these are missing, incorrect, or illegible, the payment clock doesn't start until the package is corrected and resubmitted.

The average broker receives 60–70% of invoice packages with at least one deficiency on the first submission. Every deficiency adds 5–15 business days to your payment cycle.

The Documents You Need to Send (And How to Send Them)

Rate Confirmation

Use the signed original. Not a screenshot, not a forwarded email thread — the actual signed rate confirmation as a clean PDF.

Bill of Lading (BOL)

The BOL must be: legible, signed by the consignee at delivery (not just at pickup), and show the load number that matches the rate confirmation. An unsigned BOL or missing delivery signature will stop payment automatically at most brokers.

Invoice

Your invoice must include: your MC number, your EIN, the broker's load number, pickup and delivery dates and locations, agreed rate broken down by linehaul plus any accessorials, and your payment terms.

Submit everything in one PDF, in order: Invoice → Rate Confirmation → BOL. Making it easy to process yours is how you get paid faster.

The Invoice-to-Collection System That Works

  1. Day 0 (delivery day): Driver photographs the signed BOL immediately at delivery. Uploaded to your system before the truck leaves the dock.
  2. Day 0–1: Your back office compiles the full invoice package and submits within 24 hours of delivery.
  3. Day 3: Confirm the broker received and accepted the package. If there's a deficiency, correct and resubmit same day.
  4. Day 7 (quick-pay) or Day 25 (net-30): Follow-up if payment hasn't been confirmed. Reference the load number and invoice date.
  5. Day 32+: Escalation to broker's AP supervisor if no payment and no dispute raised. Document every contact.

Accessorial Charges: The Money Most Carriers Leave Behind

Detention, layover, TONU, lumper fees, and fuel surcharge true-ups are often not invoiced because the carrier doesn't have a process for capturing them. These charges can add $200–$1,200 per load.

To collect accessorials you must: notify the broker in writing while the situation is happening, get verbal acknowledgment, and add the charge to your invoice with a reference to the notification. Brokers rarely push back on accessorials that were documented in real time.

When to Use Factoring vs. Fix Your Invoicing

Factoring is not a solution to poor invoicing — it's a liquidity tool for carriers with a working invoicing system who need cash faster than their customers pay. If your invoice rejection rate is high, factoring just means you're paying 2–4% of gross revenue for the privilege of having someone else deal with your broken documentation process.

Fix the invoicing system first. Then evaluate factoring based purely on whether the cost of capital is lower than the opportunity cost of waiting for payment.

The Back Office Advantage

Carriers with dedicated back office support collect faster than owner-operators doing their own paperwork. The difference in payment cycle between a carrier with dedicated back office support and one without is typically 15–20 days per invoice. On a 5-truck fleet running 3 loads per truck per week, that's a cash flow difference of $40,000–$80,000 permanently sitting in transit.
