Tow Truck Motor Club Billing Guide — AAA, Agero and More

Motor club work is the financial backbone of most tow truck operations — but it is also the source of the most billing frustration in the industry. Underpayments, delayed payments, disputed calls, and administrative complexity compound every month. This guide covers the billing process for the four major motor clubs and the specific practices that get tow operators paid correctly and on time.

How Motor Club Billing Actually Works

When you complete a motor club call, you are not billing the motorist — you are billing the motor club (AAA, Agero, Urgently, HONK, or others) directly. The motor club pays you at a pre-negotiated rate schedule, not at your standard retail rate. Understanding this distinction is the foundation of everything else in motor club billing.

Your rate schedule is set when you become a network provider. Most motor clubs have standard rate schedules by region with limited room to negotiate for new providers. As your call volume increases and your performance metrics improve, your leverage to negotiate better rates increases — but only if you ask.

AAA Billing: What You Need to Know

AAA is the largest motor club network in North America with over 60 million members. AAA billing runs through the AAA provider portal and requires specific documentation for each call:

  • Service call number (assigned by AAA dispatch)
  • Member name and membership number as confirmed at the scene
  • Vehicle description — year, make, model, color
  • Pickup location with coordinates or verified address
  • Destination address
  • Service type performed (tow, tire change, lockout, fuel delivery, jump start)
  • Mileage — from pickup to destination, with any extra mileage clearly documented
  • Service completion time

AAA's biggest billing complexity is extra mileage. Your rate schedule includes a base tow distance — typically 5 to 10 miles depending on your market. Miles beyond that are billed at a per-mile rate. Always verify the actual tow distance against AAA's mapping system before submitting — AAA uses their own route calculation, not Google Maps, and discrepancies are common in rural areas.

AAA regional clubs are semi-independent. The billing portal, rate schedule, and documentation requirements can differ between AAA Northeast, AAA Southern California, AAA Mid-Atlantic, and others. Confirm requirements with each club directly when you enroll as a provider.

Agero Billing: Portal and Documentation

Agero is a major motor club administrator that handles roadside assistance programs for dozens of insurance companies and automakers — including programs you may know under other brand names. When you accept an Agero dispatch, you are often actually serving a member of Ford Roadside Assistance, Hyundai Motor Club, or another affiliated program.

Agero billing runs through the Agero provider portal. Required documentation:

  • Service event number from the Agero dispatch
  • Customer and vehicle information as provided by Agero
  • Photos of vehicle at pickup — this is increasingly required and reduces dispute risk significantly
  • GPS confirmation of pickup and drop-off locations
  • Any accessories or extras (dollies, winch, skates) documented with reason for use

Agero disputes are most commonly triggered by mileage discrepancies and accessory charges. For accessories specifically — document why the item was required at the scene, not after the fact. "Vehicle was in ditch requiring winch recovery" documented at dispatch time is nearly undisputable. The same charge added to an invoice two days later is routinely denied.

Urgently and HONK: The Digital-First Motor Clubs

Urgently and HONK operate as technology platforms that connect motorists with service providers. Both use app-based dispatch and require providers to maintain active app presence to receive calls.

Key differences from traditional clubs:

  • All documentation is submitted through the app — photos, GPS confirmation, and completion confirmation are built into the dispatch flow
  • Payment is typically faster than traditional clubs — some operators report payment within 48 to 72 hours versus 15 to 30 days for AAA and Agero
  • Rate schedules are generally lower than AAA and Agero in most markets
  • App response time is critical — calls are routed to the nearest provider who accepts within a short window, typically 60 to 90 seconds

For operators focused on volume, Urgently and HONK can fill schedule gaps effectively. For operators focused on revenue per call, traditional clubs typically pay more per job.

The Accessorial Charges Most Operators Miss

Motor club base rates rarely reflect the true cost of a complex service call. These accessorial charges are billable under most rate schedules but are frequently not submitted:

  • Winch recovery: Vehicles off the roadway requiring winch use should trigger an additional charge. Document every winch use at dispatch time.
  • Dollies: Required when towing all-wheel drive vehicles or vehicles with locked steering. Most clubs pay a flat dolly rate — know yours.
  • Extra mileage: Anything beyond your contractual base tow distance. This is the most consistently underbilled line item in motor club billing.
  • After-hours premiums: Some clubs pay a premium for after-hours calls. Know your rate schedule and whether this applies to your contract.
  • Rotator or specialized equipment: For heavy recoveries where specialized equipment was required. These require prior authorization from the club in most cases.

Why Motor Club Payments Get Denied or Reduced

The most common reasons for motor club billing denials:

  • Mileage claimed does not match the club's routing calculation — always document actual GPS route
  • Missing or illegible photos — photograph every vehicle at pickup, every delivery, and any damage or special conditions
  • Service completed before authorization was confirmed — always get a service event number before starting work
  • Incorrect service type coded — a lockout billed as a tow will be denied
  • Member information mismatch — verify member name and number at the scene
  • Accessorial charges added without documentation of reason

Building a Motor Club Billing System That Pays Every Time

The operators who get paid correctly and consistently have built their billing into a system — not a task they do when they get around to it. Core elements of a working motor club billing system:

  • Submit all calls within 24 hours of completion — most clubs have a submission window and late submissions are denied
  • Photograph every call at pickup and delivery — make it a non-negotiable driver habit
  • Reconcile your dispatched calls against submitted calls weekly — missing a call is lost revenue
  • Track payment status per call and follow up on anything unpaid after 21 days
  • Document all accessorial use at dispatch time, not billing time
  • Review your rate schedules annually and request renegotiation if your volume justifies it

Motor club billing is not complex — it is repetitive. The operators who lose money on motor club work are almost never losing it to fraudulent denials. They are losing it to missed extras, late submissions, and underdocumented calls that could have been paid with one more photo taken at the scene.
