Should You Outsource Your Trucking Back Office? The Honest Cost-Benefit Analysis

Haulxify Team · February 11, 2025 · 7 min read

The logistics back office outsourcing conversation usually starts in the wrong place. Operators hear "outsourcing" and think call centre, language barriers, and lost control. The conversation should start with a number: $68,500.

That's the all-in annual cost of one mid-level back office employee in the US — salary, payroll taxes, benefits, workers' comp, and the overhead space they occupy.

75%
Typical cost reduction when replacing US-based back office staff with an outsourced logistics operations team. The work doesn't decrease — the cost does.

What "Back Office" Actually Includes

  • Load finding and dispatch
  • Document processing (BOL, POD, rate confirmations)
  • Invoicing and accounts receivable
  • IFTA tracking and filing
  • Driver qualification file management
  • Insurance certificate management
  • Carrier packet completions for new brokers
  • Bookkeeping and expense categorisation

In a 5-truck operation, this is easily 40+ hours of work per week. This work doesn't disappear when you don't have staff — it gets done badly, by drivers and owners at odd hours, or it doesn't get done at all.

The True Cost of In-House Back Office

For a 5-truck fleet with one dedicated back office employee:

  • Salary: $42,000–$52,000
  • Payroll taxes (~8%): $3,400–$4,200
  • Health insurance contribution: $6,000–$9,000
  • Workers' comp: $800–$1,400
  • PTO and holidays: $2,400–$3,000
  • Training and turnover cost: $4,000–$8,000 amortised
  • Total: $58,600–$77,600 per year

That same coverage from a logistics outsourcing provider for a 5-truck fleet: $15,000–$24,000 per year.

What You Actually Give Up

A properly structured outsourcing arrangement gives you: a dedicated team who learn your operation, coverage continuity, no HR burden, and scalability. The things you genuinely give up: the ability to physically supervise the team and some flexibility in real-time direction. Whether they outweigh the cost difference depends on your operation.

The Break-Even Analysis by Fleet Size

For a single-truck owner-operator doing their own back office, outsourcing makes sense if the time freed up generates more than $500–$700/month in additional load revenue. One additional load per week at average linehaul rates achieves this easily.

For 3–10 truck fleets, the cost comparison is unambiguous. The outsourced cost is 60–75% lower than in-house, the coverage is more consistent, and the freed management time typically generates returns that far exceed the savings.

For fleets above 20 trucks, the calculus changes. You likely have enough volume to justify a dedicated in-house team — but even at this scale, specialist functions like IFTA filing and compliance management often remain more economically outsourced.

What to Ask Before Signing With Any Back Office Provider

  • Will I have dedicated team members, or am I in a shared pool?
  • What is your staff turnover rate, and what happens to my account when someone leaves?
  • Can I speak directly with my team, and via which channels?
  • What's your error correction process when something goes wrong?
  • What platforms and TMS systems have you worked with?
  • Is there a minimum contract length, and what are the exit terms?

The right provider gives you clear, direct answers to all of these. Vague answers about "our team" and "our processes" without specifics are a warning sign.
