How Much Does Truck Dispatch Service Cost? (Complete Pricing Breakdown)

Haulxify Team · March 18, 2025 · 9 min read

If you are researching truck dispatch services, the first question is always the same: what does it actually cost? The answer varies significantly depending on the type of service, how it is priced, and what is included. This article breaks down every pricing model in the market so you can compare accurately.

$68,500
Average all-in annual cost of one US-based back office employee — salary, taxes, benefits, workers' comp, and overhead. This is the baseline most owner-operators are unknowingly comparing dispatch services against.

The Three Main Dispatch Pricing Models

1. Percentage of Gross Revenue

Many independent dispatchers charge 5% to 10% of the gross load revenue they book for you. On a $2,500 load, a 7% fee is $175. This model aligns incentives — the dispatcher earns more when they book better rates — but it can get expensive fast on high-value freight.

The risk: some dispatchers prioritize high-gross loads over loads that fit your equipment and lanes efficiently. A $3,000 load that puts your truck in a dead freight area may cost you more in total than a $2,200 load that repositions you perfectly.

2. Flat Rate Per Load

Some dispatchers charge a flat fee per load dispatched — typically $50 to $150 per load depending on freight type and complexity. This model is predictable and easy to budget. It works well for high-volume, lower-rate freight like dry van where loads are relatively standard.

The risk: flat-rate dispatchers have little incentive to negotiate aggressively on rate, since their fee is the same regardless of what the load pays.

3. Per Vehicle Per Day (Back Office Model)

Full-service back office providers — including dispatch, billing, document processing, and compliance support — typically price by vehicle per day or per month. This is the most comprehensive model and the most cost-effective for operators who need more than just load finding.

Current market rates for this model range from $3.50 to $8.00 per vehicle per day depending on the provider, services included, and whether they are US-based or offshore. This translates to roughly $100 to $240 per vehicle per month.

What Should Be Included in a Dispatch Service

Before comparing prices, confirm exactly what each service includes. Minimum expectations for a full-service dispatch arrangement:

  • Load finding across DAT, Truckstop, and direct broker channels
  • Rate negotiation on your behalf
  • Rate confirmation review and booking
  • Driver communication and load updates
  • Check calls and delivery confirmation
  • BOL and POD collection and processing
  • Invoice preparation and submission
  • Basic compliance document tracking

Services that are sometimes included and sometimes add-ons: IFTA filing, bookkeeping, after-hours coverage, factoring coordination, and driver hiring support. Clarify these before signing anything.

US-Based vs Offshore Dispatch: The Real Cost Difference

A US-based independent dispatcher typically charges 8% to 12% of gross revenue. On a truck running $15,000 in gross freight per month, that is $1,200 to $1,800 per month — or $14,400 to $21,600 per year, per truck.

An offshore back office provider offering equivalent services typically costs $100 to $150 per vehicle per month — or $1,200 to $1,800 per year, per truck. The annual cost difference on a 5-truck fleet is $60,000 to $90,000.

The quality gap that once justified the price premium has largely closed. Logistics back office operations in Pakistan, India, and the Philippines have trained specifically on US freight workflows, ELD platforms, load boards, and broker portals. The question is not whether offshore dispatch is competent — it is whether the specific provider you are evaluating has the right training and experience.

Questions to ask any dispatch provider before hiring: Which load boards do you use? What TMS platforms have you worked with? Do I have a dedicated dispatcher or am I in a shared pool? What is your average response time for driver communication? What happens to my account if my dispatcher leaves?

Hidden Costs to Watch For

The advertised rate is rarely the all-in cost. Watch for these additions:

  • Setup or onboarding fees: Some providers charge $200 to $500 to set up your account. Legitimate operations do not charge this.
  • Minimum volume requirements: Some percentage-based dispatchers require a minimum number of loads per month or a minimum gross revenue threshold.
  • After-hours surcharges: If overnight coverage is important to your operation, confirm whether it is included or billed separately.
  • Contract lock-in: Be cautious of 6 or 12-month contracts with exit penalties. Month-to-month arrangements are standard among reputable providers.
  • Per-call or per-document fees: Some lower-headline-rate providers bill for every individual task. A $3/call rate sounds small until you run the math on a busy week.

What a 5-Truck Fleet Actually Pays

To make this concrete, here is what a 5-truck dry van operation running regional freight typically pays under each model:

  • Percentage model at 8%: $75,000 gross monthly revenue × 8% = $6,000/month, $72,000/year
  • Flat rate at $75/load, 4 loads/truck/week: 20 loads × $75 = $1,500/month, $18,000/year
  • Per vehicle back office at $3.50/day: 5 trucks × $3.50 × 30 days = $525/month, $6,300/year
  • In-house dispatcher (1 employee): $58,000 to $77,000/year all-in

The right choice depends on your volume, freight type, and how much back office support you need beyond pure dispatch. But the cost difference between models is significant enough to warrant a detailed comparison before committing.

The Right Question Is Not "What Does It Cost" — It Is "What Does It Cost Per Load"

Dispatch service cost only matters relative to what it produces. A dispatcher charging 10% who consistently books loads 15% above the lane average is cheaper in net terms than a dispatcher charging 6% who books at or below average. Track your rate per mile before and after switching providers — that number tells you far more than the service fee alone.
